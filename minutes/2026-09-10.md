### Minutes:

**Consultation with Wei Tian (Tutor)**
- Flagged inconsistency in the Group Project Plan: introduction and literature review read as short term forecasting, while other sections reference medium to long term, with one section mentioning half hourly forecasting
- Advised the team should commit to one specific forecast horizon rather than spanning medium to long term, since different horizons require different models and variables
- Confirmed it's acceptable to refine the forecast horizon between the Project Plan and Group Report, provided the change is clearly justified
- Rough definitions given: short term is hours to days, medium term is weeks to months, long term is more than about a year (no strict standard, team should define and stay consistent)
- Advised the literature review should be tightened to studies matching the chosen horizon, and used to establish a benchmark for comparison
- On features: confirmed assumptions (e.g. around future temperature) are acceptable but must be justified using literature or evidence from the data, not assumed by default
- Confirmed it's reasonable to simplify scope (fewer models, fewer feature variants) given time constraints, as long as trade offs are acknowledged
- Confirmed the team's current task planner approach (data engineering/EDA, then per person model assignment, then selecting and refining the best model) is reasonable

**Forecast Horizon Decision (Team Discussion)**
- Reviewed Arman's completed feature engineering pipeline (half hour, hour, day of week, month, day of year, is weekend, is public holiday, is school holiday, UTC offset, trend years, sine/cosine harmonics, heating/cooling degree) and Faiza's completed EDA (data quality, descriptive statistics, outliers, MSTL decomposition, autocorrelation, stationarity testing), both confirmed to support any of the three horizons
- Agreed to change forecast horizon from medium to long term to short term, based on alignment with existing features, stronger fit with the literature review, and the ability to benchmark against AEMO's official forecast (up to around 40 hours ahead)
- Agreed this change will be explained and justified in the final Group Report as a deliberate shift from the original Project Plan

**Scope Simplification**
- Agreed to drop solar radiation as a planned feature due to added complexity and lack of a reliable data source
- Agreed to keep public holiday and school holiday as features, since both remain relevant to short term prediction
- Agreed against pursuing further external datasets, given sufficient data already available for a strong short term analysis
- Swapnil to reframe his literature review section (shifting from long term rooftop solar trends to short term demand trend analysis)

**Data & EDA Consolidation**
- Arman to merge the cleaned demand/temperature dataset with public holiday and school holiday data into a single consolidated dataset, to be pushed to GitHub
- Agreed no separate EDA needed on the holiday datasets individually, since they'll be merged as simple features
- Faiza and Swapnil to work from a single shared EDA file rather than two separate ones, to avoid duplication in the final report
